Name cloud

Bubble area = distinct works. Click a name; scroll to zoom.

How is this counted?

Each bubble is a credited name with a non-empty label. Its area is the number of distinct works the name is credited on, in any of the selected roles; several credits on one work count once. Its colour is the role with the most of those works.

Names loaded takes the names with the most works first. With All, the busiest 20,000 are packed exactly and drawn first, and the rest are placed in rings by work count around them as they arrive.

A work is a record typed fiaf:MonographicWork or fiaf:Work; the Datasets filter keeps works with an identifier from the chosen identifier authorities.

Timeline

Works by production year. Four-digit years only. Click a bar for titles.

How is this counted?

Works per production year: the first four digits of each production event date. A work dated in several years counts once in each year. Works with no four-digit production date are left out; the dated share above says how many.

Streams split the decade counts by genre, form, credited role, production country or language usage. Each stream is the number of dated works with that value in each decade; a work with several values counts under each, so the streams can add up to more than the decade's works. Colour, base material, element type and item format count items instead, as in the heatmaps: the items of works produced in that decade, whether the value is recorded on the item or on its manifestation. Item formats are grouped by their format class in the FIAFcore ontology (35mm film is a film format, MXF a digital format): each class has one hue, its formats are shades of it, and beyond four formats a class's smallest are combined.The largest seven values have their own stream and the rest are combined under Other. Volume centres the streams around a moving baseline; Share stacks each decade to 100%.

A work is a record typed fiaf:MonographicWork or fiaf:Work; the Datasets filter keeps works with an identifier from the chosen identifier authorities.

How is this counted?

Each country shows the distinct works that list it as a production country. A co-production counts once under each of its countries, so the country totals add up to more than the number of works, and works with no production country are in the Works total but not on the map.

Credited names are names with a non-empty label credited on a work in any role. Production years are the first four digits of production event dates; dates without a four-digit year are left out.

A work is a record typed fiaf:MonographicWork or fiaf:Work; the Datasets filter keeps works with an identifier from the chosen identifier authorities.

Per year shows the works with a production date in that year; Cumulative adds up every year so far, so a country keeps its colour once it has works. Only dated works take part, and the line above the map says how many of the works that is. A work dated in two years counts in both. The colour scale and spike heights are fixed for the whole run, so a colour or a height means the same number in every year. The bars under the map show the dated works per year; click or drag along them, or focus them and use the arrow keys (Page Up and Page Down jump ten years), to pick a year; Back and Next step one year at a time.

Spikes rise from the centre of each country's largest landmass, so a large country does not look busier just because it covers more of the map. Their height is on a log scale: each step in the legend is ten times the works of the one before, so a country with a tenth of the works is not ten times shorter. Countries with no works in the frame have no spike.

Heatmaps

Production countries by decade. Click a cell for titles.

How is this counted?

Each matrix counts distinct works. Decades are four-digit production years, and a co-production counts under each production country. A spoken language can be recorded on the work, its manifestation or an item, and subtitles on the manifestation or an item; the work counts whichever level carries it.

Each matrix counts items, except Items per work, which counts works by how many items they hold. Colour, sound, base, element and format can be recorded on an item or on its manifestation; both count as items. Decades are four-digit production years, and a co-production counts under each production country.

A work or item with several values counts once under each, so row and column totals can be larger than the distinct works or items. % of column and % of row divide each cell by its column or row total. Categories are ranked by their total, and Max categories keeps the top ones.

Co-production countries can also be drawn as a chord: each arc is a country, sized by the works it shares with the other countries shown, and each ribbon is a pair, as wide as the works the two share. A work made by three countries counts once in each of its three pairs. The chord shows at most 40 countries.

The other matrices can be drawn as a mosaic: each row category becomes a column as wide as its total, split by height into its share of each column category. The mosaic shows at most 20 columns and eight colours; decades keep their order and are shaded from early to late, other categories beyond the seventh are combined under Other.

A work is a record typed fiaf:MonographicWork or fiaf:Work; the Datasets filter keeps works with an identifier from the chosen identifier authorities.

Breakdowns

How works are distributed by production country, spoken language, genre and form.

Item distributions for format, element type, base material, colour, sound, film stock and holding institution. Flow follows the items across several of these at once.

How is this counted?

Each panel counts distinct works per value. A co-production counts under each of its countries, and a work with several spoken languages, genres or forms counts once under each, so a panel's bars can add up to more than its works. A spoken language can be recorded on the work, its manifestation or an item. Each panel lists its most common values.

Flow counts the same items across holding institution, format, element, base, colour and status at once. Each column shows its eight most common values, then Other and Not recorded; ribbons between two columns carry the items that share both values and are coloured by the first column. Hover or click a block to follow its items through every column. Format, element and colour fall back to the manifestation when the item has none. An item with two values on one column (two formats, say) counts under both, so the total can be slightly above the number of items.

Each panel counts distinct items per value, whether the value is recorded on the item or on its manifestation. An item with several values counts once under each, so a panel's bars can add up to more than its items. Each panel lists its most common values.

Recorded preservation coverage

Works with at least one item explicitly marked for preservation. This view describes recorded holdings, not whether a work survives.

Group by

Preservation status can belong to any item of a work.

How is this counted?

A work counts as preserved when at least one of its items is marked with preservation status (fiaf:hasStatus fiaf:Preservation). FIAFcore records preservation on items, not manifestations. Formats, codecs and element types do not imply preservation status.

Recorded data only. Neither missing preservation status nor missing item records means a work is unpreserved; coverage reflects cataloguing as well as holdings.

Coverage is preserved works as a share of all works, or of works with at least one recorded item. By decade, a work dated in several decades counts in each; by country, a co-production counts under each country. “Works with nitrate holdings recorded” keeps only works with a nitrate-base item or manifestation.

A work is a record typed fiaf:MonographicWork or fiaf:Work; the Datasets filter keeps works with an identifier from the chosen identifier authorities.

Kilometres of film

How much physical film the collections hold, by production decade, base and element. Click a block to zoom in.

How is this counted?

Lengths are recorded on manifestations (fiaf:hasExtent with a fiaf:Metre value), not on single prints or negatives. Each item of a manifestation is a complete element of the film, such as a release print, a duplicate negative or a sound negative, so each physical film item counts the manifestation's full length. An item counts as physical film when it has a base or a film format; digital files are left out.

Some manifestations repeat the same length on several extent records; it counts once. Extents recorded in minutes are durations and are left out, as is any length over 50 km, which would be a data error. A manifestation records one length, so a 16 mm copy of a film measured on 35 mm is an approximation.

The projection time assumes 35 mm at 24 frames a second (27.36 metres a minute). Decades come from the production date; works without one are under No production year. Today only the Bundesarchiv records lengths. The Datasets filter applies.

Roles

Roles sized by works; outer ring shows the busiest names per role. Click a role to filter the cloud.

How is this counted?

The inner ring has one arc per credited role, sized by the distinct works with at least one credit in that role. The outer ring shows the 8 busiest loaded names in each role, sized by their works in that role; a name with several roles appears under each.

The outer ring only draws from the names loaded with Names, so a larger slice can change who appears there; the inner ring always counts every credit.

Combinations counts every credited name, not only the loaded ones. Each arc is a role and each ribbon a pair of roles, as wide as the names credited in both, on any works. A name in three roles counts once in each of its three pairs, so an arc adds up to more than its names. Only the roles chosen in the Roles filter take part. Beyond 14 roles, the ones with fewest combining names share an Other roles arc.

Most roles lists the 50 names credited in the most distinct roles, whatever the Roles filter, with each name's distinct works in each role; names tied at the end of the list are cut by identifier. Unlabelled names are left out.

A work is a record typed fiaf:MonographicWork or fiaf:Work; the Datasets filter keeps works with an identifier from the chosen identifier authorities.

Ontology

The FIAFcore schema, read from the graph itself. The class tree shows every class and how many records use it; the schema graph shows what the properties connect. Click a class or a connection for details.

How is this counted?

Classes and their descriptions come from the FIAFcore ontology in the graph (owl:Class, rdfs:subClassOf, dc:description). A record counts for the class it is typed with and for every class above it, so Activity includes every Director and Cast Member. Records are typed with their most specific class only; the handful that carry two types count under both. Many classes are used as values rather than types: no record is typed fiaf:Germany, but works name it as their production country, so the tree also counts how often each class is used as a value. Classes used neither way are shown as faint outlines.

Size by classes gives every class the same height, so the whole ontology is visible; size by use sizes each class by its records plus its uses as a value, and unused classes disappear.

The schema graph groups classes under their top-level class (Cast Member under Activity, Germany under Country). Each arrow is one property between two top-level classes. Counting every triple's types is too heavy for the public endpoint, so the view samples up to 500 triples per property, reads the types at both ends, and scales the sample by the property's real triple count: arrow weights are estimates. Values that are classes themselves, such as a country or a film format, are drawn as dashed arrows. Properties with literal values (dates, title text, identifier values) are listed with their class instead of drawn.

Counts cover the whole public graph. The Datasets filter does not apply here.
